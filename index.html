Why the falcon

The peregrine conquers the edge in the air.

It's the fastest animal alive not through brute strength, but through a dive — folding in, aiming precisely, and committing to the edge instead of avoiding it. That's the model for how MELL teaches: not more hours, a sharper line of attack.

Wits tells its students, "Wits gives you the edge." Most students experience that edge as the thing they're afraid of — the difficulty, the pace, the fear of falling behind. MELL's position is that you go toward it, on purpose, with someone who's already dived.

MELL is named after Meluleki — a mentor whose belief was that high-quality education should be within reach of any student willing to put in the work, not just the ones who can already afford it.

How a session runs

The method

  • LearnThe concept is taught as a reusable system, not a one-off walkthrough.
  • TryApplied immediately to a real past-paper question, under time.
  • FailMistakes surface fast, in the room, where they're cheap to fix.
  • RelearnThe gap gets named precisely and closed — no vague "review this again."
  • Try againRepeat on a new question until it holds under exam pressure.
